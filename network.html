HANDSHAKE PROTOCOL — WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS
  1. Broadcast a null request. Not your handle. A string with the shape of a request and no content in it. Any node that answers is either ours or wants us to think it is.
  2. Wait exactly long enough to be wrong. Two seconds is standard. Answering faster than two seconds is how the Inquisition traces a relay.
  3. Rotate the source header. Present the address of a node two hops away, which is a node that does not exist. If the reply comes back signed by that address, someone has been reading the mesh for months.
  4. Never authenticate first. A relay that asks for your credentials before it has shown you a valid signature is a Hunter relay. Close the session and burn the address.
  5. Read the last packet, not the first. Our relays always append four bytes of padding to the final block. Second Inquisition relays are tidy. Tidy is wrong.
  6. Assume the room is listening, then talk normally. The act of being careful is louder than the thing you were careful about.
